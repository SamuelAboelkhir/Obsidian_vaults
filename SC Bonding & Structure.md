# Chemical bonds
## Intro
- These are strong electrostatic forces holding atoms or ions together, which are formed by the rearrangement (transfer or sharing) of outermost electrons
- Atoms seek to achieve the electronic configuration of the nearest noble gas **_The Octet Rule_** via chemical bonds
- Atoms can achieve such a state by obtaining full outer electron shells, either by gaining electrons to complete the shell, or losing them to lose it all together
	- That's how atoms become **_ions_**
- Ions are electrically charged since they contain an unequal amount of protons and electrons
- When an ion has more protons, it's positively charged, and is called a **_cation_**
- When an ion has more electrons, it's negatively charged, and is called an **_anion_**
- [[SC Coordination Chemistry#Transition elements/metals|Transition metals]] can have multiple types of ions, but some of them, like [Zn, Cd, Hg] from the 12th group and Ag from the 11th group, have only one 
	- Ag -> $Ag^+$
	- Zn -> $Zn^{2+}$ or $Zn^{++}$
- By losing different numbers of electrons, mot transition metals can create different types of ions though
- For example
	- Copper can be $Cu^+$ like in copper (I) oxide - $Cu_2O$ or $Cu^{2+}$ like in copper (II) oxide ($Cu^{2+}$) - $CuO$
	- Iron can also be $Fe^{2+}$ like in iron (II) chloride - $FeCl_2$  or $Fe^{3+}$ like in iron (III) chloride - $FeCl_3$
## Types of bonds
- Atoms can bond in 3 different ways:
	- **Ionic Bonds**: Metals with non-metals
	- **Covalent bonds**: Non-metals only
	- **Metallic bond**: Metals only
- To re-iterate, atoms with an incomplete outer shell, may be uncharged, but they're also unstable, which is why bonding takes place

### On bonds

- **Covalent bonds** with <u>unequal sharing</u> of electrons have an ionic character
- Most of the times, chemical bonds are actually **_intermeiate_** between ionic and covalent
## Ionic Compounds
- An ionic compound is any compound that contains ions
- They're usually formed from the reactions between metals and non-metals
- The reaction results in both atoms having full outer shells, rendering them highly stable
- The resulting positive and negative ions are strongly attracted to each other, due to electrostatic attraction, forming an ionic bond
- This means that the reason a molecule like NaCl exists is because the Na atom gives its outer electron to the Cl atom, making them both stable AND charged $Na^+$ and $Cl^-$ which are then attracted to each other because of the electrostatic charges
- The most stable and by consequence inert elements out there are noble gases
- This degree of stability is what other atoms are always aiming to achieve
- For sodium with the electron configuration (2, 8, 1), that was by losing that last sole 3S orbital electron
- For chlorine, it was the other way around, (2, 8, 7), it was to gain an electron to fill its last 3P orbital

## Formation of Ionic Bonds
- Generally speaking, atoms of groups IA and IIA, the alkali metals and alkaline earth metals tend to reach the noble gas structure of the previous period by losing their outermost electron
- The formation of positive ions for those metals is actually endothermic and not spontaneous, which means that it takes energy for this to happen
- Atoms of groups VIA and VIIA need the opposite, to gain electrons, and this, IS an exothermic and spontaneous process. It releases energy
- **Electron Affinity (E.A)** is a measure of the energy released when an electron is added to an atom
- It's seen from the atom's perspective, so a negative number indicates high affinity because that's the amount of energy lost from the atom
- Group VIA and VIIA atoms always have a negative E.A for the first electron they gain
### Ion formation for cations
- Cations are large, and have low **Ionization Energy (I.E)**, which is a measure of the energy required to lose an electron
- A low I.E means that it's easy for the atom to lose an electron
- Therefore, atoms of group IA and IIA form cations easily
- The general trend is that as you move down a group and to the start of a period, the ease of forming a cation increases. The fact atoms grow larger is also a contributor, since the increase in distance between an electron and the nucleus, makes the loss of the electron easier
### Ion formation in anions
- These atoms are smaller than the rest
- They have more negative E.A. making the formation of anions easier
- The general trend here is the opposite from cation forming atoms. As we go up in groups and down in periods, atoms grow smaller, making it easier for them to gain electrons
## Lattice Energy (U)
- This is the energy released from the formation of 1 mole of an ionic crystalline compound from its constituents in their gaseous state
- Example:
	- $Na^+(g) + Cl^-(g) -> NaCl(S)$
	- $Mg^+(g) + 2Cl^-(g) -> MaCl_2(S)$
- Lattice energy is affected by the following:
	- **Ionic size**: The greater the ionic size, the lower the lattice energy
		- That makes it less negative since it's energy release, like E.A
	- **Ionic charge**: The greater the charge, the higher the lattice energy

## Properties of ionic compounds
- An ionic compound is formed of millions of ions that are packed together in a regular cubic arrangement that are joined by ionic bonds
- They form a 3D structure called an **_ionic lattice_**
![[Pasted image 20261001223932.png]]
- The structure of the lattice means the ionic compound is forming a crystal, with flat sides and straight edges
- Here is a photo of NaCl crystals
![[image.HFUKW3.png|528]]
- **All** ionic compounds form such lattices and crystals when solid
- Ionic bonds are quite strong, requiring a lot of heat to break, making them solid at **room temperature**
- The larger the ionic charge the stronger the ionic bond

| Compound        | Ion charges | Melting point ($C^°$) | Boiling point ($C^°$) |
| --------------- | ----------- | --------------------- | --------------------- |
| Sodium Chloride | 1+ and 1-   | 801                   | 1,413                 |
| Magnesium Oxide | 2+ and 2-   | 2,852                 | 3,600                 |
- Ionic compounds don't conduct electricity in their solid form as the ions are packed and unable to move
- When molten, the lattice breaks up, and the ions are free to move, allowing them to carry an electric current, due to them being charged particles
- They're also soluble in water since water molecules have a slight electrical charge that can attract the ions away from the lattice
- Again, they can carry an electric charge when dissolved since they're free to move
- They're also brittle and shatter if force is applied
