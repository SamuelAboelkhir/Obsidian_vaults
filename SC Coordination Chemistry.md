# Transition elements/metals
## Atomic orbital filling in the periodic table
![[image.MJNDW3.png|555]]
- A transition element is an element whose atom in the ground state or ion in the common oxidation state has an incomplete d-sub-shell, (n-1)d1-9
- They are so-called transition elements because they lie in between the most electropositive block (s-block) and the most electronegative block (p-block) on the periodic table, so they represent a transition between them
- $(n-1)d^{1 to 10} ns^{0 to 2}$
- This doesn't include [Zn, Cd, or Hg] as they have complete d orbitals in both ground and common oxidation states
- Their common oxidation state is $Zn^{++}$ and so on, and they don't show the characteristics of transition elements
- Transition metals are 
	- Malleable and ductile
	- Good conductors of electricity and heat
	- At least one of their ions and compounds is colored
	- Form coordination compounds
	- Show variable oxidation states
