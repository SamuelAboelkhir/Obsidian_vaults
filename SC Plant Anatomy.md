## **1. Introduction to Plant Anatomy**
- **Definition:** Plant anatomy is a foundational science critical for understanding plant structure, function, and adaptation.
- **Key Focus:**
  - Study of plant elements and tissues to comprehend adaptations to specialized functions and environmental conditions.
  - Emphasis on the structure, variability, and form of plant tissues.
- **Angiosperms (Flowering Plants):**
  - Most recently evolved plant group.
  - Dominant in natural and cultivated vegetation.
- **Plant Embryo Components:**
  - Root growing point, shoot growing point, and cotyledons.
- **Growth Mechanism:**
  - New cells form via meristematic (embryonic) tissue at growing points.
  - Cells undergo growth and differentiation to develop into mature tissues.

---

## **2. Plant Cell Structure**
- **Basic Components:**
  - **Cell Wall:** Box-like structure surrounding the protoplasm.
  - **Protoplasm:** Contains organelles (nucleus, mitochondria, plastids) and cytoplasm.
- **Protoplasmic Contents:**
  - **Cytoplasm:** Fluid matrix of the cell.
  - **Plasma Membrane:** Regulates material exchange.
  - **Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER):** Protein and lipid synthesis.
  - **Golgi Apparatus:** Transport and modification of molecules.
  - **Mitochondria:** Energy production (ATP).
  - **Plastids:** Include chloroplasts (photosynthesis), chromoplasts (color), and leucoplasts (storage).
  - **Ribosomes:** Protein synthesis.
  - **Nucleus:** Genetic material and cellular control.
- **Non-Protoplasmic Contents:**
  - **Vacuoles:** Store water, ions, and waste products.
  - **Reserve Materials:**
    - **Carbohydrates:** Sugars, inulin, glycogen, cellulose, pectins, gums, starch.
    - **Proteins:** Stored in specialized cells.
    - **Fats/Oils:** Energy reserves.
  - **Excretory Materials:** Calcium oxalate and carbonate crystals.

## **3. Meristematic Cells**
- **Definition:** Embryonic cells responsible for plant growth via continuous cell division.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Thin-walled, isodiametric shape, rich in protoplasm, large nuclei.
  - Lack reserve materials and crystals; plastids are in proplastid stage.
  - Small, scattered vacuoles.
- **Development Stages:**
  - **Meristematic Cells → Mature Cells:** Progressively older cells (A → C).
- **Classification by Position:**
  1. **Apical Meristems:** At shoot and root tips.
  2. **Intercalary Meristems:** At internode bases.
  3. **Lateral Meristems:** Parallel to organ surfaces (e.g., vascular cambium).
- **Classification by Origin:**
  1. **Primary Meristems:** Directly derived from embryonic cells.
     - Develop epidermis, cortex, mesophyll, and primary vascular tissues.
  2. **Secondary Meristems:** From differentiated mature tissues.
     - Examples: Phellogen (forms bark), vascular cambium (forms secondary xylem/phloem).

## **4. Differentiation and Tissue Maturation**
- **Differentiation Process:**
  - Meristematic cells undergo growth and specialization to form mature tissues.
  - Loss of embryonic traits and acquisition of specialized functions.
- **Mature Tissues:**
  - Permanent tissues (e.g., epidermis, cortex, phloem) with fixed structures and functions.

---

## **5. Tissue Classification**
- **Types of Tissues:**
  1. **Meristematic (Embryonic):** Active dividing cells.
  2. **Mature (Adult):** Specialized for specific functions.
- **Functional Classification:**
  1. **Protective Tissues:**
     - **Dermal Tissue:** Outer covering (epidermis, cuticle, bark).
       - **Epidermis:** Protects against water loss, contains stomata and trichomes.
       - **Cuticle:** Waxy layer; absent in aquatic plants and rhizodermis.
       - **Stomata:** Gas exchange structures (guard cells, subsidiary cells).
         - **Shape:** Kidney-shaped (dicots) or dumbbell-shaped (monocots).
         - **Substomatal Chamber:** Air space for gas exchange.
  2. **Fundamental Tissue (Parenchyma):**
     - Living cells with thin walls; involved in photosynthesis, storage, and secretion.
     - **Types:**
       - **Chlorenchyma:** Contains chloroplasts (photosynthesis).
       - **Storage Parenchyma:** Stores carbohydrates, proteins, oils.
       - **Secretory Parenchyma:** Excretes waste (e.g., calcium oxalate crystals).
  3. **Supporting Tissues:**
     - **Collenchyma:**
       - Living cells with unevenly thickened primary walls (cellulosic).
       - Provides structural support in young organs (stems, leaves).
       - Walls thicken in response to wind exposure.
       - May resume meristematic activity under stress.
       - Absent in most monocots.

---

## **6. Epidermal Cells and Stomata**
- **Epidermal Cell Shapes:**
  - Isodiametric (barrel-shaped), elongated (palisade or bulliform), cubical.
- **Cuticle Variability:**
  - Thickness and structure vary with environmental conditions.
  - Examples: Thin (some plants), thick (desert species), absent (aquatic plants).
- **Stomatal Structure:**
  - **Guard Cells:** Regulate stomatal opening (kidney-shaped or dumbbell-shaped).
  - **Subsidiary Cells:** Functionally associated with guard cells.
  - **Substomatal Chamber:** Air space for CO₂ and O₂ exchange.

# Plant Anatomy: Cells, Tissues, and Vascular Systems  

## Types of Cells and Tissues  

### Collenchyma  
Collenchyma is a supporting tissue with thickened cell walls. Four main types are distinguished:
- **Angular Collenchyma**:
  - Thickening occurs in the angles of the cell wall.
  - In cross-section, thickenings are seen where three or more cells meet.
- **Lamellar Collenchyma**:
  - Thickening is mainly on the tangential walls of the cells.
- **Lacunar Collenchyma**:
  - Thickening occurs in areas facing intercellular spaces.
  - Some intercellular spaces may be visible.

### Sclerenchyma
Sclerenchyma consists of dead cells at maturity with thickened secondary walls (often lignified). It is divided into two types:
- **Fibers**:
  - Long, lignified cells with simple pits.
  - Commonly found in vascular tissue and ground tissue.
- **Sclereids**:
  - Short, branched cells (brachy-sclereids or stone cells).
  - May have reduced lumens and branched canal pits.
  - Found in stems, leaves, and fruits.

### Supporting Tissue
- **Sclerenchyma** provides structural support and protection.
- **Fibers** and **sclereids** are the two main forms, differing in cell shape and origin (fibers from meristematic cells, sclereids from parenchyma cells).

---

## Vascular Tissue

### Xylem
- **Function**: Transports water and minerals.
- **Primary Xylem**:
  - Formed by the procambium in the primary plant body.
  - Divided into **protoxylem** (earlier-developing, smaller vessels) and **metaxylem** (later-developing, larger vessels).
- **Secondary Xylem**:
  - Formed by the vascular cambium in the secondary plant body.
- **Structure**:
  - Composed of **tracheary elements** (tracheids and vessel members) and **atrateary elements** (fibers and parenchyma).
  - Tracheary elements are non-living and specialized for transport.

### Phloem
- **Function**: Transports photosynthetic products.
- **Primary Phloem**:
  - Develops from the procambium.
- **Secondary Phloem**:
  - Develops from the vascular cambium.
- **Structure**:
  - Composed of **sieve elements** (sieve cells or sieve-tube members), **companion cells**, **parenchyma**, and **fibers**.
  - Sieve elements are the conducting cells of the phloem.

---

## Arrangement of Vascular Tissue

### Types of Vascular Arrangements
1. **Radial Arrangement**:
   - Xylem and phloem form alternating arches on different radii.
   - Example: Monocot and dicot roots.
2. **Complete Cylinder**:
   - A full cylinder of cambial cells between xylem and phloem cylinders.
   - Example: Some dicot stems.
3. **Vascular Bundles**:
   - Xylem and phloem are arranged as discrete units in the ground tissue.
   - Example: Monocot and dicot stems.

### Types of Vascular Bundles
1. **Opened Collateral Vascular Bundle**:
   - Cambium is present between xylem and phloem.
   - Example: Dicot stems.
2. **Bi-Collateral Vascular Bundle**:
   - Inner phloem develops alongside outer phloem.
   - Example: Some dicot stems.
3. **Closed Collateral Vascular Bundle**:
   - No cambium; xylem and phloem on the same radius.
   - Example: Monocot stems.
4. **Radial Vascular Bundle**:
   - Xylem and phloem alternate on different radii.

---

## Primary Growth of Stems

### Dicot Stem
- **Structure**:
  - **Epidermis**: Covered with cuticle, includes guard cells and trichomes.
  - **Cortex**: Composed of parenchyma, collenchyma, and sclerenchyma (fibers/sclereids).
  - **Vascular Tissue**: Arranged in one or two rings.
    - **Xylem**: Endarch (protoxylem inward, metaxylem outward).
    - **Phloem**: Contains parenchyma and sieve elements.
  - **Pith**: Parenchyma with occasional sclereids.
  - **Pith Rays**: Parenchyma cells.
- **Features**:
  - Presence of **cambium** for secondary growth.
  - Opened collateral vascular bundles.

### Monocot Stem
- **Structure**:
  - **Ground Tissue**: No distinct cortex or pith; vascular bundles are scattered.
  - **Vascular Bundles**: Closed collateral, surrounded by a bundle sheath of fibers.
  - **Xylem**: Endarch, with protoxylem vessels forming a Y- or V-shaped arrangement.
- **Features**:
  - No cambium; vascular bundles are fixed in position.
  - Protoxylem cavity may form in rapid elongation (e.g., *Zea mays*).

---

## Key Differences Between Dicot and Monocot Stems

| Feature                | Dicot Stem                          | Monocot Stem                          |
|------------------------|-------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| **Cortex & Pith**     | Distinct cortex and pith           | No distinct cortex or pith           |
| **Vascular Arrangement** | Vascular bundles in rings         | Scattered vascular bundles           |
| **Cambium**            | Present                            | Absent                               |
| **Vascular Bundle Type** | Opened collateral                 | Closed collateral                    |
| **Phloem Parenchyma**  | Present                            | Absent                               |
| **Bundle Sheath**      | Absent                             | Present                              |
| **Xylem Shape**        | Endarch (protoxylem inward)        | Endarch (Y- or V-shaped)             |

# **Primary Plant Body Anatomy: Roots and Leaves**
*Notes from Plant Anatomy Course (Fall 2017)*

---

## **II. Root Primary Growth**
### **A. Tissue Development**
- **Protoderm** develops the **rhizodermis** (outer root layer) with no cuticle. Some protoderm cells form **unicellular root hairs**.
- **Ground meristem** produces the **cortex**:
  - Primarily **parenchyma** cells.
  - Occasionally includes **sclerenchyma** cells.
- **Endodermis** is the innermost cortex layer:
  - Cell walls are thin, except for the **casparian strip** (a suberized band):
    - **Dicots**: Suberized on **radial walls**.
    - **Monocots**: Suberized on **radial and transverse (inner tangential) walls**.
- **Vascular cylinder (stele)** develops from the **procambium** and forms the root’s central core.

### **B. Vascular Tissue Organization**
- **Proto-xylem** (first matured xylem elements) are **exarch** (directed outward).
- **Phloem** forms in spaces between protoxylem arms.

### **C. Dicot vs. Monocot Roots**
| Feature                | **Dicot Roots**                          | **Monocot Roots**                          |
|------------------------|------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| **Pith**               | Narrow, limited                          | Wide, central parenchyma (pith)            |
| **Xylem Arrangement**  | Metaxylem in middle of stele            | Multiple xylem arches (>5), termed **poly-arch** |
| **Vascular Bundle Count** | 2–8 arches (di-arch to tri-arch)     | >8 arches (poly-arch)                      |
| **Pericycle**          | Meristematic, involved in lateral root formation | Meristematic, same role as dicots         |

### **D. Key Structures**
- **Pericycle**: Outer layer of the stele; meristematic, critical for lateral root formation.
- **Casparian Strip**: Regulates water and solute movement into the vascular cylinder.

---

## **III. Leaf Primary Growth**
### **A. Principal Tissues**
1. **Epidermis**:
   - Covers leaf surfaces (upper and lower).
   - **Cuticle** on outer surfaces.
   - **Stomata** (gas exchange pores):
     - Dicots: Guard cells are **kidney-shaped**.
     - Monocots: Guard cells are **kidney- or dumbbell-shaped**.
2. **Mesophyll**:
   - Photosynthetic parenchyma tissue between epidermis layers.
   - **Chloroplasts** present.
   - Differentiated into:
     - **Palisade parenchyma**: Narrow cells with long axes perpendicular to the leaf surface.
     - **Spongy parenchyma**: Irregular, loosely arranged cells with large intercellular spaces.
3. **Vascular Bundles (Veins)**:
   - Network of xylem and phloem.
   - **Xylem** near upper surface; **phloem** near lower surface.
   - **Bundle sheath**: Parenchyma cells surrounding vascular bundles (may or may not contain chloroplasts).

### **B. Dicot vs. Monocot Leaves**
| Feature                | **Dicot Leaf**                          | **Monocot Leaf**                          |
|------------------------|-----------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| **Mesophyll**          | Palisade and spongy parenchyma         | Palisade (or chlorenchyma) only           |
| **Supporting Tissue**  | **Collenchyma** (flexible support)     | **Sclerenchyma** (rigid support)           |
| **Cambium**            | Present (for secondary growth)         | Absent                                    |
| **Vascular Bundles**   | **Open collateral** (xylem/phloem on same radius) | **Closed collateral** (xylem/phloem on same radius) |
| **Bundle Sheath**      | Parenchyma                            | Sclerenchyma                              |
| **Phloem Parenchyma**  | Present                               | Absent                                    |
| **Xylem/Phloem Arrangement** | Proto- and metaxylem inverted exarch on same radius | Proto- and metaxylem inverted exarch in **Y/V shape** |

---

## **Key Comparative Features**
### **Roots**
- **Dicots**:
  - Exodermis present.
  - Radial vascular bundles.
  - Casparian strip on radial walls.
- **Monocots**:
  - Exodermis present.
  - Radial and transverse walls with casparian strip.
  - Poly-arch vascular bundles.

### **Leaves**
- **Dicots**:
  - Collenchyma for support.
  - Open collateral vascular bundles.
  - Phloem parenchyma present.
- **Monocots**:
  - Sclerenchyma for support.
  - Closed collateral vascular bundles.
  - No phloem parenchyma.
