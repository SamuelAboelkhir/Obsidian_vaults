**Important Biochemical trends:**

1- Carbonic acid which is made from CO2 + H2O → H2CO3 is the main carbon dioxide donor in cells but only after dissociating into bicarbonate.

2- Bicarbonate HCO3- is the main carboxylic acid donor in cells. Carboxylase enzymes that transfer the carboxylic acid moiety from it usually contain biotin, a vitamin belonging to the vitamin B family

3- Amination is usually done via a Glutamine AA and an ATP which results in the release of Glutamate and AMP (The amino group from the R group of Glutamine is used) or an Aspartate AA and ATP with the exception of Aspartate and IMP which requires a GTP molecule instead of ATP (The amino group from the AA backbone itself is used and subsequent removal of the carbon backbone Fumarate is then required).

4- The four universal electron donors/acceptors are NAD+ NADP+ FMN+ FAD+

NAD+ and FAD+ are mainly involved in catabolic energy-producing reactions while NADP+ is involved in anabolic reactions, FMN is the first electron acceptor in Complex I ( also known as the NADH-Ubiquinone oxidoreductase ) of the electron transport chain.

Proteins that can bind these donors are usually characterized by a Rossmann Fold.

5- Alpha helices tend to have their hydrophobic residues every 3rd or 4th residue.

6- The N-terminus is translated first by ribosomes.

7- Hemoglobin aggregates in RBCs are called Heinz bodies.

8- Phosphorylation and dephosphorylation happen at the three residues serine, threonine, and tyrosine.

9- A kinase enzyme transfers phosphate from ATP to its substrate, a phosphorylase transfers inorganic phosphate to its substrate, and a phosphatase removes phosphate from its substrate.

10- The regulation of different enzymes and pathways has many forms like allosteric regulation (very fast), transient covalent modifications (slower than allosteric) that require signaling cascades to be done, and are mainly used in reciprocal regulation, transcriptional and translational regulation, and compartmentalization.

11- Disulfide bonds are used by enzymes to transfer electrons and protons by reducing the disulfide bond creating two thiol groups and then completing the transfer with the regeneration of the disulfide bond.

12- GLUT1: RBCs and brain

GLUT2: Liver (and portal area), pancreas, intestines, kidneys, and brain (hypothalamus area)

GLUT3: Brain (most prominent type, mainly in neurons), liver, kidneys, and placenta

GLUT4: Muscles and adipocytes

13- The aminotransferase enzyme replaces a carbonyl’s oxygen with NH3 and vice versa in reverse action therefore it only works with molecules that have a carbonyl.

14- Thiokinase enzymes like acyl-CoA synthetases use MG2+ as a co-enzyme and the CoA molecule always replaces the single bond oxygen of a carboxylic acid due to the formation of a thioester bond.

15- Hydra**tase** enzymes catalyze the addition or removal of a molecule of water. While hydrol**ase** enzymes catalyze the cleavage of a covalent bond using a molecule of water e.g: ester**ase**, phosphat**ase**.

16- A mutase is an isomerase enzyme that catalyzes the movement of a functional group from one position to another within the same molecule

17- A thiolase catalyzes the grouping of two Ac-CoA into one acetoacetyl-CoA while a thiokinase is a ligase, an enzyme that catalyzes the formation of a new chemical bond to join two large molecules that can combine a CoASH molecule with another molecule by forming a thioester bond between them.

18- Enzymes responsible for joining and cutting molecules to create new ones are generally termed synthet**ase**s. Synthetases-also called ligases-examples include: thiokin**ase**, thiol**ase**, hydrol**ase**, hydrat**ase**, etc…
